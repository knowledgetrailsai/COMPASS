# Canada — AI Regulation

_Last reviewed: 2026-08-19 — Canada's federal AI-specific legislative status has been in flux; confirm current status of AIDA/Bill C-27 directly before relying on it._

## Federal AI-specific legislation status

The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) was proposed as part of Bill C-27, intended to introduce risk-tiered obligations for "high-impact" AI systems broadly similar in spirit to the EU AI Act's approach. As of last review, this legislation's status (passage, further amendment, or lapse with a change in Parliament) should be verified directly rather than assumed — Canadian federal AI-specific legislation has not had the same settled trajectory as the EU AI Act.

## What's binding today regardless of AIDA's status

- **PIPEDA (Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act)**: federal private-sector data protection law, applies directly to AI systems processing personal data
- **Provincial privacy laws**: Quebec's Law 25 in particular imposes GDPR-style obligations, including specific requirements around automated decision-making (disclosure and a right to have automated decisions explained/reviewed) that are already binding and AI-relevant independent of AIDA's status
- **Sector regulators**: financial services (OSFI guidance on model risk management), human rights commissions (provincial and federal) applying existing anti-discrimination law to algorithmic decisions

## Practical guidance

Given the uncertainty around AIDA specifically, anchor Canadian compliance planning to: (1) PIPEDA/provincial privacy law as the stable binding baseline, (2) Quebec Law 25's automated-decision-making provisions as the most directly AI-relevant binding requirement currently in force, and (3) sector regulator guidance for regulated industries — treating AIDA as a "monitor for developments" item rather than a current compliance requirement until its status is confirmed.
