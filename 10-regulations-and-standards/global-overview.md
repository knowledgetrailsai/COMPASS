## Comparison table

| Jurisdiction | Primary AI-specific law | Approach | Status as of Aug 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU | AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) | Risk-tiered, horizontal (covers all sectors) | In force; high-risk system obligations recently postponed to December 2027 via the "Digital Omnibus" simplification package — see [EU/eu-ai-act.md](EU/eu-ai-act.md) |
| US | No single federal AI law; sectoral + state laws + executive branch policy | Sectoral/state patchwork | Evolving; several states (e.g., Colorado) have enacted AI-specific laws — see [US/federal.md](US/federal.md), [US/state-laws.md](US/state-laws.md) |
| India | Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 + DPDP Rules 2025 (data-protection focused, not an AI-specific law) | Data protection as the primary binding lever on AI; sectoral AI guidance from MeitY/RBI/SEBI | DPDP Rules notified November 2025; phased compliance timeline — see [India/dpdp-act.md](India/dpdp-act.md) |
| UK | No standalone AI Act; "pro-innovation," principles-based, regulator-led approach | Existing regulators (ICO, FCA, CMA, etc.) apply sector-specific guidance under cross-cutting principles | See [UK/uk-ai-regulation.md](UK/uk-ai-regulation.md) |
| China | Algorithm and generative AI-specific regulations (e.g., generative AI service measures) | Sector/technology-specific binding rules, content and security-focused | See [China/china-ai-regulation.md](China/china-ai-regulation.md) |
| Singapore | No standalone binding AI law; model governance frameworks (advisory) | Voluntary framework-led (e.g., Model AI Governance Framework), sectoral regulation via MAS for finance | See [Singapore/singapore-ai-regulation.md](Singapore/singapore-ai-regulation.md) |
| Canada | AIDA (Artificial Intelligence and Data Act) proposed as part of Bill C-27; provincial privacy laws apply now | Federal AI-specific law was pending as of last review — check current status | See [Canada/canada-ai-regulation.md](Canada/canada-ai-regulation.md) |

## Cross-cutting observations

- **Risk-tiering is the dominant regulatory pattern** — the EU AI Act's approach (unacceptable/high/limited/minimal risk) has influenced how other jurisdictions and frameworks structure obligations, including this repository's own tiering in [03-ai-governance/risk-management.md](../03-ai-governance/risk-management.md).
- **Existing data protection law is doing a lot of the current binding work** on AI even where no AI-specific law exists yet — GDPR-style and DPDP-style obligations apply directly to AI systems processing personal data regardless of whether an "AI law" exists.
- **Deadlines are moving, not disappearing** — the EU AI Act postponement is a simplification/timeline adjustment, not a repeal of obligations; treat regulatory delay as a planning input, not a reason to deprioritize compliance work.
- **Multinational organizations should design to the strictest applicable regime** (typically the EU AI Act for risk-tiering rigor, DPDP/GDPR-style rules for data handling) rather than maintaining jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction compliance variants where feasible.
