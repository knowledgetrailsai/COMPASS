# US — Federal AI Policy

_Last reviewed: 2026-08-19. The US federal AI policy landscape is unusually fluid — no single comprehensive federal AI law exists, and executive branch policy has shifted materially across administrations. Verify current status before relying on specifics here._

## No comprehensive federal AI statute

Unlike the EU, the US has no single binding, comprehensive AI law at the federal level as of this review. Federal AI governance instead operates through: executive orders and administration policy, sector-specific regulators applying existing authority to AI, and NIST's voluntary framework ([09-tools-and-frameworks/NIST-AI-RMF.md](../../09-tools-and-frameworks/NIST-AI-RMF.md)).

## Executive branch policy

Federal AI policy has been set substantially through executive order, with direction shifting across administrations — including, most recently, an executive order asserting a federal policy framework that scrutinizes and seeks to limit conflicting state-level AI regulation. This creates active tension between federal and state approaches that is not yet settled — see [state-laws.md](state-laws.md).

## Sectoral federal regulation

Existing federal regulators apply their general authority to AI within their sector without needing AI-specific statutes:
- **FTC**: unfair/deceptive practices authority applied to AI claims, bias, and consumer harm
- **EEOC**: employment discrimination law applied to AI-driven hiring/HR tools
- **FDA**: AI/ML-based medical device review
- **CFPB / banking regulators**: fair lending law (ECOA, FCRA) applied to AI credit decisioning
- **SEC**: AI-related disclosure and market-conduct rules for financial services

Treat these as the practical binding baseline for many high-stakes US AI use cases even without dedicated federal AI legislation — see [11-sector-specific-ai](../../11-sector-specific-ai/) for sector detail.

## NIST AI RMF

Voluntary, but referenced in various federal agency and procurement contexts — see [09-tools-and-frameworks/NIST-AI-RMF.md](../../09-tools-and-frameworks/NIST-AI-RMF.md).

## Practical guidance

Given the federal picture is unsettled and state law is actively contested (see [state-laws.md](state-laws.md)), organizations operating in the US should anchor primarily to sector-specific regulator authority (which is comparatively stable) and monitor federal/state developments as an ongoing compliance-tracking task rather than a one-time assessment.
