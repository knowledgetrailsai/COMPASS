# ISO/IEC 42001

## What it is

The first international standard specifying requirements for an **AI Management System (AIMS)** — analogous in structure to ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management. Organizations can be independently certified against it.

## Core structure (typical ISO management-system pattern)

- Context of the organization (understanding internal/external factors relevant to AI)
- Leadership and commitment
- Planning (risk assessment, objectives)
- Support (resources, competence, awareness, documentation)
- Operation (operational planning and control across the AI lifecycle)
- Performance evaluation (monitoring, internal audit, management review)
- Improvement (nonconformity handling, continual improvement)

## Why pursue certification

- Credible, externally verifiable signal to customers/partners/regulators that AI governance is systematic, not ad hoc
- Often a procurement requirement for enterprise/public-sector customers
- Provides structure that can satisfy elements of other regulatory obligations (though certification alone doesn't guarantee compliance with a specific law like the EU AI Act — the two are related but distinct)

## Certification process (general pattern)

1. Gap assessment against the standard's requirements
2. Implementation/remediation to close gaps
3. Internal audit
4. External certification audit by an accredited certification body (Stage 1 documentation review, Stage 2 implementation audit)
5. Ongoing surveillance audits to maintain certification

## Relationship to ISO/IEC 23894

ISO/IEC 42001 is the management-system standard (what the organization must have in place); ISO/IEC 23894 provides risk-management guidance that informs how to fulfill the risk-related requirements within it — see [ISO-23894.md](ISO-23894.md).
